# GreenDot Build & Interiors --- Brand Brief

## 1. Source of truth

This brief is derived from the supplied **GreenDot Build & Interiors •
Website & Digital Brand Discovery Brief**. The supplied document is the
primary source for brand positioning, founder information, offerings,
customer journey, FAQ direction, content pillars and copy guardrails.

The source describes GreenDot as a Chennai-focused customized
prefabricated construction + interiors company, initially serving
Chennai and South India. Its central proposition is not simply "fast
prefab"; it is a predictable, customized, coordinated way to build
spaces with clear scope, agreed cost, planned timelines and
quality-focused execution.

Source references: supplied PDF, especially pages 2--11.

## 3. Brand idea

### GREEN

Represents the brand's ambition toward more sustainable construction
solutions, efficient methods, reduced waste and responsible material
choices.

### DOT

Represents a defined project path: defined scope, planned timeline and
agreed cost.

### The combined idea

> Sustainable solutions. Defined timelines. Agreed costs. End-to-end
> execution.

This should be interpreted as a brand philosophy rather than an absolute
guarantee.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. The problem GreenDot exists to solve

The brand brief identifies recurring construction problems:

-   Costs increasing during execution and handover
-   Delays extending projects
-   High construction waste
-   Errors, rework and repairs
-   Inconsistent execution quality
-   Heavy structures creating additional foundation/structural
    requirements
-   Fragmented projects where clients coordinate multiple contractors
-   Poor predictability around cost, quality and completion

The website should therefore sell **control and clarity**, not merely
construction technology.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Positioning

### Primary positioning

**A predictable, end-to-end way to build customized spaces.**

GreenDot combines: 1. Requirement understanding 2. Site assessment 3.
Planning/design 4. Appropriate prefab system selection 5. Defined scope
and specifications 6. Quotation 7. Prefab construction 8. Interior
execution 9. Finishing 10. Handover

### Positioning statement

GreenDot Build & Interiors helps clients turn unusual, customized or
site-constrained requirements into planned, coordinated spaces using
appropriate prefabricated systems and end-to-end execution.

### What GreenDot should NOT look like

Avoid positioning the company as: - Just another prefab house company -
Just a PUF panel supplier - Just an LGSF manufacturer - Just an interior
designer - A generic civil contractor - A "cheap and fast construction"
company - A company making universal speed, durability or sustainability
guarantees

## 6. Differentiation

### 1. Customized

The solution begins with the client's requirement, available space,
purpose, site and budget.

### 2. Predictable

Scope, specifications, cost and target timeline are clarified before
execution once the project is sufficiently defined.

### 3. End-to-end

Prefab construction and interiors can be coordinated through one team.

### 4. Experienced

5+ years of practical experience and 100+ sites under supervision.

### 5. Site-ready

Experience includes urban, remote, rooftop, restricted and challenging
locations.

### 6. Efficient

Prefab is used where it creates practical advantages in time, waste,
logistics and execution.

## 7. Ideal customer groups

### A. Rooftop owners

People who have an existing terrace and want: - Extra room - Home
office - Gym - Entertainment room - Café - Guest accommodation - Utility
space - Other customized extensions

**Primary anxiety:** "Can this actually be built safely and practically
on my terrace?"

**Website response:** feasibility-first messaging, site assessment CTA,
structural/approval qualification.

### B. Café / restaurant founders

Need a space that can open efficiently and look distinctive.

**Anxiety:** delays, fragmented contractors, uncontrolled scope,
design/execution mismatch.

**Website response:** show design-to-handover journey, commercial
fit-out capability and relevant project stories.

### C. Farmhouse / hospitality owners

Need customized guest rooms, cottages, hospitality spaces or supporting
structures.

**Anxiety:** site logistics, appearance, timeline, coordination and
remote execution.

**Website response:** showcase site adaptability and end-to-end
execution.

### D. Commercial clients

Need offices, retail, gyms, entertainment spaces, commercial units or
fit-outs.

**Anxiety:** business downtime, cost escalation and contractor
coordination.

**Website response:** single-point coordination, defined scope and
planned execution.

### E. Clients with an unusual idea

They do not know which construction technology they need.

**Key message:** \> You don't need to know whether you need LGSF, PUF or
another system. Start with what you want to build.

## 11. Copy rules

### Use

-   Customized
-   Planned
-   Defined
-   Coordinated
-   Site-specific
-   Project-specific
-   Practical
-   Transparent
-   End-to-end
-   Quality-focused
-   Appropriate system
-   Agreed project cost
-   Target timeline

### Avoid unsupported absolutes

Do NOT write: - "100% quality guaranteed" - "Guaranteed 30-day
construction" - "Fixed price no matter what" - "Zero maintenance" -
"Lifetime durability" - "100% eco-friendly" - "No approvals required" -
"Can be built on every terrace" - "Cheapest prefab construction"

### 30--60 days

If mentioned, describe it only as a potential target for suitable
projects. Actual duration depends on size, complexity, approvals, site
conditions and scope.

### Rooftops

Always qualify: - Structural feasibility - Existing structure - Site
access - Dimensions - Applicable approvals

## 14. Technical SEO / Next.js requirements

### Rendering philosophy

Use a **server-first Next.js App Router architecture**.

Important distinction: - Do not force every page into
`dynamic = "force-dynamic"`. - Static, content and project pages should
be statically rendered/revalidated where possible. - Pages depending on
request-time data can use SSR. - The goal is minimal client JavaScript
and maximum server rendering, not artificial dynamic rendering
everywhere.

### Required technical practices

-   Next.js App Router
-   React Server Components by default
-   TypeScript strict mode
-   Minimal `"use client"`
-   Server-side data fetching
-   `generateMetadata`
-   `sitemap.ts`
-   `robots.ts`
-   Canonical URLs
-   Open Graph metadata
-   Twitter/X metadata where appropriate
-   JSON-LD rendered server-side
-   Organization/LocalBusiness schema only where accurate
-   Service schema where appropriate
-   BreadcrumbList
-   Article schema for genuine articles
-   FAQPage schema only when appropriate and compliant
-   `next/image`
-   Correct image sizing and responsive sources
-   AVIF/WebP where supported
-   Lazy loading below-the-fold images
-   Preload only genuinely critical hero assets
-   Accessible semantic HTML
-   Keyboard navigation
-   Focus states
-   Reduced-motion support
-   Proper heading hierarchy
-   Descriptive links
-   404 page
-   500/error handling
-   Loading states where they improve UX
-   No layout shifts
-   No unnecessary animation libraries
-   Clean internal linking
-   Pagination where needed
-   `notFound()` for missing dynamic routes
-   Stable slugs
-   Redirect handling
-   Environment variables for secrets
-   No API keys in client bundles

### Performance targets

Build toward excellent Core Web Vitals: - LCP: ideally \< 2.5s - INP:
ideally \< 200ms - CLS: ideally \< 0.1

Do not sacrifice usability or visual quality to chase arbitrary
Lighthouse scores.

## 17. Competitive opportunity

Most competing websites tend to lead with one of three propositions:

1.  **Technology:** LGSF / PUF / prefab
2.  **Design:** beautiful interiors
3.  **Turnkey execution:** one contractor from design to handover

GreenDot can occupy the intersection:

> **Customized space + appropriate prefab technology + interiors +
> predictable project experience**

The website should therefore not try to win by shouting "fastest" or
"cheapest".

It should win by making the client think:

> "They understood what I am trying to build, they can figure out the
> right construction approach, and I won't have to coordinate five
> different people."
